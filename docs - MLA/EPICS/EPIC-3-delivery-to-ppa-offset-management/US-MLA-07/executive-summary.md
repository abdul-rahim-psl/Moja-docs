# US-MLA-07 — Retry and Circuit-Break on PPA Failures: Executive Summary

**Epic:** EPIC-3 — MLA: Delivery to PPA & Offset Management
**Status:** complete — exit criterion met live, recorded in `plan.md` §16
**Date:** 2026-09-07

---

## What this story set out to achieve

The other half of US-MLA-06's offset gate: what actually happens to a non-200 response, classified correctly and handled without ever silently dropping an event or hammering a known-down PPA forever. A transient failure (5xx, timeout, TLS-handshake failure) retries with genuine jitter; a permanent one (4xx) logs the full envelope and advances immediately, because retrying a malformed envelope cannot fix it; and retry exhaustion parks the event and pauses consumption, feeding a circuit breaker that trips at a configurable threshold and — the story's own reason for existing — re-probes on a timer so a paused partition always has a path back. The POC collapsed retry and breaking into one mechanism with no MLA-side threshold at all; this story is what keeps them two coordinated mechanisms, deliberately.

## The reasoning behind the decisions that were not obvious

**`network-error` — PPA's host wholly unreachable, not just returning a bad status — was put in the same retried bucket as the three categories the story names explicitly, even though nothing in the story text says so.** The alternative reading (treat it as permanent, since it isn't named) would mean advancing the offset the instant PPA goes fully down, which is *more* final than an ordinary 503 and the exact silent-data-loss failure mode this whole mechanism exists to prevent. Nothing in `engineering-rules.md` §6.1 calls a bare connection failure permanent either — this is a reasoned default built against, not a guess buried in behaviour, and it is flagged in both `plan.md` §8 and the `plan.md` §16 entry for exactly this reason: to be raised back to CCH/COMESA rather than silently assumed correct forever.

**The circuit breaker's scope is per-partition, not process-wide — the opposite of the PII secret's own breaker, deliberately.** The PII secret is a single in-process resource: if it is broken, it is broken identically for every partition at once, so a process-wide counter reflects reality and a per-partition one would just make several partitions independently re-derive the same fact. A PPA delivery failure is different in kind — a per-call HTTP outcome that can genuinely be localized to what one corridor's own envelopes happen to trigger — so a process-wide counter would risk one troublesome partition pausing every other, unrelated one for no reason. This is an interpretation of `core-knowledge.md` §3.5's own "pause consumption on the affected partition(s)" wording (plural, selective), not a verbatim requirement, and it is consistent with reasoning already on record in this codebase from before this story existed (`circuit-breaker.service.ts`'s own comment, written to justify the PII secret's *process-wide* scope by contrast). Flagged plainly in the `plan.md` §16 entry, not buried in behaviour.

**The shared counting primitive was generalized rather than duplicated.** `PiiCircuitBreaker` was, on inspection, a pure state machine with zero PII-specific logic inside it — only its doc comments referred to PII at all. Building a second, functionally-identical class for PPA would have been a verbatim duplicate of real logic, the exact case `engineering-rules.md`'s SOLID-calibration rule asks to remove pain for. It became `CircuitBreaker` (`circuit-breaker.service.ts`), with the two domains' differing *scope* (process-wide vs. per-partition) composed on top rather than baked into the primitive: the PII secret's own composition root now injects one process-wide `CircuitBreaker` instance directly, and `PpaCircuitBreaker` (`ppa-circuit-breaker.service.ts`) wraps one such instance per partition behind a small, lazily-populated map.

**A reprobe's own `client-error` result resumes the partition and resets the breaker, exactly like a success does.** A 4xx surfacing on a reprobe (PPA has come back up but now permanently rejects this specific envelope) is itself proof of connectivity — there is nothing systemic left to keep the partition paused for, even though this one event's own outcome is still a permanent failure that gets logged and advanced past. Drawing this distinction (three-way reprobe resolution, not the PII secret's own two-way one) is what R-22/this story's own AC require: the 4xx-advances rule and the transient-retries rule both still apply exactly as they would on a first attempt, never suspended just because the failure happened to surface mid-reprobe instead.

## What was proven live, versus assumed

Every mechanism this story built was exercised against a real, running `ppa-stub` and a real broker, with re-signed real captured records, across two live-verification sessions. Jitter was proven twice, independently, with visibly different real timings each time (2739ms/3943ms/2772ms for a hung connection; 864ms/1417ms/1508ms for a persistent 503) — not merely asserted from a mocked clock. A 4xx produced exactly one delivery attempt, no retry, and an alert carrying the full serialized envelope including its tokenized fields, live confirmation Phase 4's tokenization survives this path unchanged. A genuinely untrusted client cert was retried like a 5xx and its alert named the handshake specifically, never a generic "HTTP 5xx." And the story's own central claim — a paused partition always has a path back — was proven the way that matters most: a persistent 503 against a real running instance tripped the breaker at exactly its configured threshold (5 consecutive failures, logged once, never re-logged), and restoring `ppa-stub` health, **with no process restart**, recovered the exact parked record automatically on the very next reprobe tick. This is the one recovery path in this whole phase that did not need a restart to prove — every other recovery proof in US-MLA-06 needed one, and gate item #1's own PII reprobe *could not* be live-proven without one, since its secret client caches its answer for the process's life. Per-partition isolation (one partition's own trip never affecting another) is unit-verified only, not live — the one real capture fixture in this repo is entirely partition 2, and fabricating non-captured multi-partition data was judged not worth trading the "use real captures" discipline for. Full numbers and exact log lines: `plan.md` §16's own US-MLA-07 entry.

## What was deliberately left out

Nothing within this story's own acceptance criteria — every clause of the phase's exit criterion is met. Two things stay explicitly open past this story's own close, both already decided defaults rather than blanks: the circuit-breaker threshold (N=5, `plan.md` §8.1 #2) and the offset-advance-on-permanent-failure policy (`plan.md` §8.1 #3) are both formally unconfirmed with CCH. Phase 6 is what turns the structured log lines this story produces into real metrics and wired alerts — nothing here claims more than the interim log-line posture every phase before it has taken.

## What this exposed that outlives it

**A precedent from an adjacent, similar-looking mechanism is a starting hypothesis, not an answer, even inside the same codebase.** The PII secret's retry/park/breaker shape (gate item #1) was the explicit, named template for this story's own mechanism — and it was right to reuse the retry-burst and reprobe *shapes* directly. But reusing the PII breaker's *scope* (process-wide) without re-examining whether PPA's own failure domain actually shares the PII secret's "broken for everyone identically" property would have been the wrong simplification, silently. The value of building this story second, after its own template already existed, was in having a second, comparably serious use case to test the first design's own reasoning against — and finding the reasoning did not fully transfer is what the resulting per-partition/process-wide split is worth recording, not treating as an inconsistency to smooth over.
